USCG Navigation Lights & Shapes

A pedagogical guide for mastering day shapes and night lights for your Master's License.

1. Power-Driven Vessels (Underway)

Under 50 Meters

  • One Masthead light (White)
  • Sidelights (Red/Green)
  • Sternlight (White)

Day Shape: None

Over 50 Meters

  • Two Masthead lights (White) - Fwd is lower
  • Sidelights (Red/Green)
  • Sternlight (White)

Day Shape: None

Rule of Thumb: "One white light high, small guy. Two white lights high, big guy." If you see two masthead lights, the vessel is over 50 meters long.

2. Sailing & Rowing Vessels

  • Sidelights & Sternlight ONLY (No masthead white light)
  • Optional at top of mast: Red over Green
  • Day Shape (if using motor): Cone apex pointing DOWN
Mnemonic: "Red over Green, Sailing Machine."
Mnemonic (Shape): "Cone pointing down, engines are around." (Means they are motorsailing and considered a power vessel).

3. Fishing & Trawling

Fishing (Lines/Nets)

  • Red over White
  • Sidelights/Sternlight (only if making way)
Mnemonic: "Red over White, Frying Fish Tonight."

Trawling (Dragging net)

  • Green over White
  • Sidelights/Sternlight (only if making way)
Mnemonic: "Green over White, Trawling Tonight."

Day Shape for BOTH: Two cones with apexes together (looks like an hourglass). Remember: "Hourglass catches fish."

4. Not Under Command (NUC)

A vessel that through some exceptional circumstance (engine failure, steering loss) is unable to maneuver.

  • Two all-round Red lights in a vertical line.
  • No masthead light.
  • Sidelights & sternlight ONLY if making way through the water.
Mnemonic: "Red over Red, the Captain is Dead." (Or "Engines are dead").
Day Shape: "Ball over Ball, can't steer at all."

5. Restricted in Ability to Maneuver (RAM)

Vessels doing underwater work, laying cable, buoy tending, or dredging. They can steer, but their work restricts them.

  • Red - White - Red in a vertical line.
  • Masthead, sidelights, and sternlight apply when underway.
Mnemonic: "Red, White, Red, working ahead."
Day Shape: Ball - Diamond - Ball.

6. Constrained by Draft (CBD) - INTERNATIONAL ONLY

Vessels so deep in the water they cannot deviate from the channel. Note: This status does not exist in Inland rules.

  • Three all-round Red lights in a vertical line.
  • Plus standard power-driven vessel lights.
Mnemonic: "Three reds in a row, no room below."
Day Shape: A single black cylinder. ("Looks like a deep can").

7. Towing & Pushing

Look at the masthead lights and the stern of the towing vessel.

Yellow Towing Light

Over

If towing astern, they display a yellow light OVER the white sternlight.

"Yellow over White, my tow line is tight."

Pushing Ahead

If pushing ahead on Inland waters, they display two yellow lights on the stern.

"Yellow over Yellow, I'm a pushing fellow."

Masthead Lights (White lights on the front mast)

  • Two white lights: Tow is less than 200 meters long.
  • Three white lights: Tow is over 200 meters long. (Day shape: Diamond).

8. Anchored & Aground

Anchored

  • Under 50m: One all-round white light.
  • Over 50m: Two all-round white lights (fwd is higher).
  • Over 100m: Must also illuminate decks.

Day Shape: One Black Ball

Aground

+ Anchor Lights
  • Anchor lights PLUS Two vertical Red lights.
Mnemonic: "Ball, Ball, Ball... I'm not going anywhere at all."

9. Pilot Vessels

  • White over Red.
  • Sidelights and sternlight apply when underway.
Mnemonic: "White over Red, Pilot Ahead." (Or "White hat, red nose").
Day Shape: None specifically for pilotage, but they fly the "H" (Hotel) flag: White/Red split vertically.
